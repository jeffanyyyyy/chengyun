01 — About Us

Cheng — clarity, made transparent.
Yun — the ground worked, step by step.

In this industry, asymmetry of information is the most expensive cost of all. Companies spend heavily and still get a rough result, because the work has been subcontracted down through layer after layer; meanwhile the performers on stage and the technicians on the front line meet squeezed rates and deductions nobody explains. Chengyun was founded to change that.

We run on the discipline of corporate management, with a modular, transparent pricing system that breaks event costs and service specifications down scientifically — giving corporate clients a clear, standardised process with real control over budget. Our work spans large corporate annual events and brand launches, high-end visual and physical craft design, integration of local artisans and art IP, and low-carbon event delivery.

We work founder-led, backed by a network of senior specialists. Precise project control and a deep visual standard carry a project from planning through specification to delivery on site — one team, end to end.

However lavish an event looks, without sound trust and dignity in labour it is a tower built on sand. Chengyun stands on integrity and transparency, and on execution done slowly and carefully, so that every brand appearance creates both the highest return and lasting value.

05 — Q&A

The questions we are asked most.

01　Services & Planning
How do you price an event?

We plan the scale and direction of an event around your budget. Every event is different, and each proposal is made to order.

Is there a minimum budget? Will you take small jobs?

Chengyun takes on projects from NT$10,000 to over NT$10,000,000. Tell us what you need and we will come back to you quickly.

Does the quote include scripting?

Some packages cover basic linking segments only. A full host script, run sheet and prize-draw mechanics may be quoted separately, listed line by line.

Does it include rehearsal?

How long the team is on site for rehearsal, and whether blocking and cue checks are included, is written down explicitly.

How is overtime calculated?

Whether the increment is half-hourly or hourly, and whether waiting time for executives counts, is defined with you before the quotation is issued.

02　Hosting & Coordination
How do you work with the rest of the crew to keep an event on track?

Behind a successful event there is more than a good host: there is close coordination with sound, lighting and the rest of the technical crew.

  1. Brief in advance — walk through the run sheet, entrances and exits, and the timing of music and lighting cues; share the script.
  2. Rehearse on site — simulate the real thing, check sound, lighting and stage blocking, and solve problems early.
  3. Keep talking during the event — radios, hand signals or agreed cues, so the team can adapt as things move.
  4. Respect professional judgement — trust the technical crew on their own equipment, and work through problems together.
03　Band & Sound
The venue already has a sound system — can the band use that instead?

Hardware is the backstage soul of a live band. A dedicated engineer and a crew used to the same equipment handle the room, the balance across frequencies and the effects for the whole event. Venue systems usually cannot meet a band's needs — controlling the character of each instrument, for one — and if equipment is damaged, liability becomes unclear. So every performance runs on the band's own professional hardware.

Why do we need an equipment upgrade above 20 tables? Can we skip it?

Up to 20 tables, the standard setup is two monitors on stage and two speakers out front — four in total. Above 20 tables without an upgrade, the front speakers are pushed too loud while guests at the back cannot hear. We recommend adding two more front-of-house speakers so the sound is even across the room.

Can we choose all the music ourselves?

A band's set is normally built from songs the singers and players know well, which is what keeps the standard up. For a particular favourite, tell us at least two months ahead so the band has time to rehearse it. If the band cannot perform it, we suggest burning it to MP3 for the DJ to play at three points: the welcome, the table toasts and the send-off.

04　Visual Design
What do you need from us to start a visual design?

First, clarify what you need. After an initial conversation about style, send us copy, images, links, proposals and any other reference material.

How many rounds of revision are included?

Design projects include four rounds of proofing. Beyond four, additional design fees may apply.

  1. Round one (major) — style and layout of the first draft. If the first draft misses the mark, a second version can be requested, with a clear direction given.
  2. Round two — replacing content within the agreed direction: images, text, assets.
  3. Round three — final precise detail adjustments; the overall direction is fixed by this point.
  4. Round four — a last check before release for typos and fine visual details, then sign-off.
We don't like either of the two initial designs. Can we have another?

At kick-off we present two visual directions based on the brief. If neither works, a design fee applies from the third version onward.
